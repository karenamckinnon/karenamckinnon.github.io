Karen McKinnon

Associate Professor, UCLA → JHU (Nov 2026)

Karen McKinnon is a professor of Statistics and the Environment at the University of California, Los Angeles. Beginning in November 2026, she will join the Department of Environmental Health and Engineering in the Whiting School of Engineering at Johns Hopkins University. Her research sits at the nexus of climate change and statistics, and is aimed at improving our understanding and prediction of climate extremes, variability, and change. She is a 2027 American Meteorological Society Henry G. Houghton Awardee, a 2024 NSF CAREER awardee, a 2023 Kavli Fellow, and a 2021 Packard Fellow in Science and Engineering, and she has served as an advisor for both city governments and private companies regarding climate change. Prof. McKinnon received her B.A., M.A., and Ph.D. from Harvard University, as well as an M.Sc. from Victoria University of Wellington. She was an Advanced Study Program post-doc at the National Center for Atmospheric Research and an Applied Scientist at Descartes Labs before joining UCLA.

Fucheng Yang

Postdoctoral scholar, Institute of the Environment and Sustainability

Fucheng Yang is a postdoctoral scholar at the University of California, Los Angeles. His previous research focused on advancing the understanding of the seasonal cycle of temperature and improving model representations of hydrological variability. He is broadly interested in how atmospheric dynamics and land-surface processes interact to shape hydrological and thermal variability across temporal scales. His current work investigates the mechanisms that control heat extremes over land from both energetic and dynamical perspectives.

Information for all prospective group members

Research in the Climate Intelligence Lab is on the physical climate system, including the development of novel statistical and machine learning methodologies to answer scientific questions about the climate system. A strong quantitative background (including in statistics, computer science, physics, and applied math) is strongly encouraged, and is more important than a background in earth or atmospheric science specifically. Research areas include weather extremes (heat, humidity, precipitation), wildfire, internal variability across timescales, land/atmosphere interactions, and the climate response to human influences.

Prospective graduate students

I am currently seeking two fully-funded PhD students to join the Lab at Johns Hopkins University! One of the positions will focus on variability and extremes in AI weather and climate emulators, and could include additional foci on uncertainty quantification, mechanistic evolution of weather extremes, and/or S2S predictions. The second position will focus on climatic controls and projections of western US hydroclimate, including wildfire, drought, and streamflow. The research will rely on developing predictive, causal models using historical observations, and identifying robust future changes using climate models. Please see the full ad here for more information and relevant qualifications. Apply through the Whiting School of Engineering Environmental Engineering program. I will interview strong candidates in the winter.

Prospective post-docs

I am currently hiring a postdoctoral fellow to work at the intersection of AI and climate. Please see the full ad here for more information. In addition, I am happy to discuss serving as a host for postdoctoral fellowships if you would like to spend time in the Lab. For those interested in climate change impacts to ecosystems, the USGS Climate Adaptation Postdoctoral Fellows Program may also be of interest. For those abroad, there are also country-specific mobility schemes to support research in the US, such as the SNSF Postdoc.Mobility for those in Switzerland and the Canada Postdoctoral Research Award program for Canadians.
